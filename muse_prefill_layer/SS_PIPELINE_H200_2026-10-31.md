## Concrete architecture

The original kernel's two idle producer warps now dequantize weights while
the two consumer warpgroups issue shared/shared BF16 WGMMA. DMA and publisher
warps retain their roles. The dequantizer loads **all** packed code/scale
fragments into registers before a 64-thread named barrier permits any
in-place overwrite. It reuses the parent's exact convert8/scale arithmetic,
then stores the BF16 fragments to an SW128 shared A operand. Every writer
executes a proxy fence and arrives at a decoded-ready barrier; consumers
acquire that barrier before asynchronous reads. Original all-consumer
slot releases still guard reuse. Metadata queue reader counts rise256->320.

Four 49,152-byte slots hold up to32KiB X plus16KiB decoded A. The old
packed4608-byte region aliases decoded A after the read barrier. Epilogue
staging shrinks45,056->34,816 bytes, sufficient for the existing G1 N*128
and G2 half-tile128*272 layouts. Including1024 bytes of metadata, total is
exactly232,448, the existing dynamic shared request. No extra global pass,
weight format, activation permutation, precision or allocation is introduced.
This is an ordering/layout design, not a racecheck or measured speedup.

The proposed overlap is related to producer/consumer specialization in
[CUTLASS](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/media/docs/cpp/efficient_gemm.md)
and [humans&'s producer-owned NVFP4 dequantization](https://humansand.ai/blog/faster-than-light-nvfp4-decode).
The latter uses Blackwell native conversions and TMEM, which H200 lacks.
This independent implementation instead reuses the incumbent Hopper decode
and aliases packed/expanded shared storage. Their reported performance does
not transfer here. NVIDIA specifies [WGMMA fragments/shared layouts](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html#asynchronous-warpgroup-level-matrix-instructions)
and [async proxy fences](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html#parallel-synchronization-and-communication-instructions-membar).
